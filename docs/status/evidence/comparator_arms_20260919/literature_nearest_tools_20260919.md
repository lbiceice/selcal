## 2. Circular and time-shift surrogates: minimum shift, resolution, and overlap with the lag range

| # | Citation | Status | Relevant quote / finding |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2.1 | Harris KD. "A Shift Test for Independence in Generic Time Series." arXiv:2012.06862 (2020). https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2012.06862 | VERIFIED (full PDF) | "If the series are independent, the unshifted value is in the top m shifted values with probability at most m/(N+1). For large N, the probability approaches m/(2N+1). A conservative test rejects independence at significance α if the unshifted value is in the top α(N+1)". The worked example: "we took N = 19, allowing the null to be conservatively rejected at p=0.05 if m = 1". **The resolution limit is explicit: with 2N+1 shifts the conservative p cannot go below 1/(N+1).** Harris uses linear (truncated) shifts, not circular ones. |
| 2.2 | Yuan & Shou 2024 PLOS Biol (see 1.2) | VERIFIED | "B is bounded between 1 and 2r+1 ... If r is exactly 19, significance will be detected at the α = 0.05 level only when B = 1, meaning that the unshifted correlation would need to exceed all shifted correlations." Also: "p naive is not a valid p-value because the surrogate y series are not independent of each other (e.g., 2 consecutive shifts are nearly identical)." For the lag range: "With a larger range of possible lags, the minimum r value increases and longer series may be needed." Their cyclic-permutation baseline uses every shift: "If the original time series was of length n, then n-1 cyclic permutation surrogate were produced." |
| 2.3 | Harris 2020 bioRxiv (see 1.11) | VERIFIED | The circular shift test "tests the null hypothesis that not only are the series independent, but one of them is also cyclo-stationary ... unless the start and end values of the timeseries are identical, cyclic shifting will introduce a discontinuity". |
| 2.4 | Cannistra et al. 2025 eLife (see 1.1) | VERIFIED (main text only) | They use all possible circular shifts with tailored lags, and p = (1 + n_larger)/(1 + n_surr). The Fig. 2 legend states the resolution floor in general form: "(in practice, at least 19 surrogates are needed to achieve p = 0.05)". I did not find the lag-overlap floor in the main text. The Appendix was not read. |
| 2.5 | Lancaster G, Iatsenko D, Pidde A, Ticcinelli V, Stefanovska A. "Surrogate data for hypothesis testing of physical systems." Phys Rep 748:1–60 (2018). https://doi.org/10.1016/j.physrep.2018.06.001 | VERIFIED (abstract) / UNVERIFIED (section on time-shifted surrogates) | This is a comprehensive review covering time-shifted and cyclic surrogates for coupling tests. A search snippet suggests shifts should exceed the correlation or embedding scale, but I did not read the relevant section. |
| 2.6 | Andrzejak RG, Kraskov A, Stögbauer H, Mormann F, Kreuz T. "Bivariate surrogate techniques: necessity, strengths, and caveats." Phys Rev E 68:066202 (2003). https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.68.066202 | VERIFIED (abstract) / UNVERIFIED (body) | The abstract promises "pitfalls and limitations" of bivariate surrogates. I did not verify body text on minimum shift. |

**Is the "p floor ≈ L/n" limitation known?** No source I checked states SelCal's specific mechanism. That mechanism: when shifts with |s| < L are included, the shifted source at shift s evaluated at lag ℓ equals the observed source at lag ℓ+s. The observed maximum then reappears in about L surrogate states, so the exact p is at least about L/n. The general ingredients are documented:
- (a) Shift or permutation resolution: the smallest p is about 1/(N+1) (Harris 2020 arXiv; Yuan & Shou 2024; IDTxl/TRENTOOL code, section 3).
- (b) Adjacent shifts are nearly identical, so shift surrogates are dependent (Bartlett via Yuan & Shou 2024).
- (c) A wider lag range needs a larger shift radius / longer series (Yuan & Shou 2024, multi-lag TTS with Bonferroni).

Cannistra et al. 2025 combine circular shifts over all shifts with tailored (re-selected) lags, which is SelCal's configuration. So they must face the same ceiling, but their main text does not name it. SelCal can present the L/n ceiling as a formalization or diagnostic of these known ingredients. Do not claim it is entirely new before checking the Cannistra Appendix and the Yuan & Shou S1 Text (sections 2–3).

| **IDTxl** (`idtxl/stats.py`, `idtxl/data.py`, github.com/pwollstadt/IDTxl master) | VERIFIED (code) | **Partial.** `max_statistic` is documented as: "Test if a transfer entropy value is significantly bigger than the maximum values obtained from surrogates of all remanining candidates." The greedy conditioning set is also not re-run. | **Default: no across time.** The default permutes replications ("the temporal ordering of samples stays intact"). If there are too few replications it falls back with "WARNING: Number of replications is not sufficient ... Permuting samples in time instead." and `perm_type` defaults to `'random'` (i.i.d.). The options `'circular'` (needs `max_shift`), `'block'` and `'local'` exist. **The circular shift has no minimum shift:** `shift = np.random.randint(low=1, high=max_shift + 1)`, so shifts of 1 are allowed. | **Yes, for resolution only.** `check_n_perm`: "The number of permutations {0} is to small to test the requested alpha level {1}. **Bottom line for section 3:**
- No tool checked redoes the full lag selection jointly inside each surrogate for the lagged-Pearson/TE max statistic. IDTxl comes closest, with a max over candidate lags that permutes each candidate independently.
- The tools that warn about resolution (IDTxl, TRENTOOL, and SciPy's exact-test note) warn only in terms of the number of permutations versus 1/α.
- **None warns that small circular shifts overlapping the lag search range reduce the number of effectively distinct surrogates.** IDTxl's circular mode allows shift = 1 with no lower bound.

## Follow-ups before citing
1. Read the Cannistra et al. Appendix (circular-shift procedure, handling of all shifts under tailored lags) to confirm whether they note the lag-overlap floor.
2. Read the Yuan & Shou 2024 S1 Text sections 2–3 (naive TTS variants, proofs).
3. Get the Olden & Neff 2001 abstract from the publisher before quoting it (currently UNVERIFIED).
4. Optionally read the Lancaster et al. 2018 section on time-shifted surrogates for any minimum-shift guidance.
